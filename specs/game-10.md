# Bill, Bowl, Bluff

## **Title**
**Bill, Bowl, Bluff**

## **Inspiration**
1. **[The mating dance of the Satin Bowerbird](https://academy.allaboutbirds.org/female-satin-bowerbird-visiting-bower/)**  
   Male bowerbirds do not just dance; they build a decorated viewing lane and then perform inside it for a judging audience. I took the idea of courtship as stagecraft, where the arena itself is part of the move set and the viewer's comfort matters as much as raw spectacle.

2. **[Why the platypus "breaks" mammal expectations](https://www.britannica.com/story/why-is-the-platypus-a-mammal)**  
   The platypus feels like taxonomy refusing to stay in one box: egg-laying, milk-producing, electro-sensing, venomous, duck-billed. I took that contradiction and made the player cycle through incompatible body modes, so identity itself becomes a tactical resource instead of a fixed character sheet.

3. **[The glass harmonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_harmonica)**  
   Wet fingers on spinning glass create eerie, sweet tones that feel hard to locate in space. I took that ghostly wet-glass resonance and turned arranged decorations into playable sound objects that fire in sequence when the player struts through the runway.

## **Elevator Pitch**
You are an impossible suitor on a tiny forest stage, building a bower runway out of glowing glass junk while an unseen judge watches from the far end. In `Bill, Bowl, Bluff`, you sprint around placing decorations, wetting them, and then bursting into short ritualized struts that cause the entire runway to sing in cascading harmonics. The catch is that your body keeps flipping between mutually contradictory platypus-like modes, and each mode changes how the same arrangement behaves. The game is a 60-second panic of interior decorating, instrument playing, and seductive lying.

## **Core Mechanics**
- **Goal**  
  Score as highly as possible in `60` seconds by making the judge lean in, stay engaged, and witness high-value display phrases without getting startled into fleeing.

- **Playfield**  
  Single-screen top-down bower lane. The judge sits at the top center, partially obscured. The player works in the lower two-thirds of the screen, arranging objects along a central display corridor.

- **Objects**  
  Decorations spawn at the edges and drift inward: glass bowls, shells, berries, blue scraps, white bones, pebbles. Each has a size, color, and resonance value. Objects can be placed anywhere, but orderly perspective gradients score better than clutter.

- **Controls**  
  `Arrow keys` move.  
  `Z` pick up or drop the nearest object.  
  `X` perform a display burst in the facing direction.  
  `C` cycle anatomy modes: `BILL`, `FUR`, `EGG`, `SPUR`.

- **Arrangement play**  
  The judge likes deliberate visual lanes. Small objects near the judge and larger objects farther away create strong forced-perspective bonuses, while neat color runs create stronger harmonic chains. Messy piles block movement and make the performance look desperate.

- **Display bursts**  
  Pressing `X` makes the creature strut, bow, and quiver for a short committed burst. Any wetted object touched by the display wave rings in order of distance from the player, so one well-built lane becomes a cascading visible melody line.

- **Body modes**  
  `BILL`: reveals faint arrows showing the current best perspective line for one second and lets display waves curve slightly toward nearby objects.  
  `FUR`: charges resonating objects so matching colors chain farther than normal.  
  `EGG`: each footstep leaves a wet patch; wetted objects can ring and stay active for a few seconds.  
  `SPUR`: creates a sharp, high-scoring accent burst that doubles nearby resonance but spikes the judge's alarm meter.

- **Judge behavior**  
  The judge has two visible meters: `INTEREST` and `ALARM`. Beautiful perspective, coherent resonance, and varied but controlled anatomy use raise `INTEREST`. Overusing `SPUR`, performing into clutter, or getting too close too suddenly raises `ALARM`.

- **Win/Lose conditions**  
  The run normally ends at `60` seconds. If `ALARM` fills, the judge bolts and the run ends immediately. There is no traditional health bar; the risk is social failure.

## **The Twist**
The novelty is that the player is not merely arranging objects or performing a rhythm pattern. They are building a one-use instrument for an audience, then changing species mid-performance to reinterpret that same instrument four different ways. A line of bowls that was decorative in `EGG` mode becomes a ghost chord in `FUR` mode and a dangerous intimidation flourish in `SPUR` mode. The scoring object is not the items, and not the dance, but the judged contradiction between them.

## **Visual Style**
Lurid natural-history cabaret. The whole game should look like a forest floor trying to impersonate an opera stage: wet blacks, petrol blues, fungus pinks, bone white, and occasional surgical gold. The player creature should read as an elegant biological mistake made of simple shapes, with its silhouette changing slightly in each anatomy mode. Resonance should appear as thin neon contour rings and trembling line-work, while good perspective layouts make the center lane feel unnaturally deep even on a flat canvas.

## **Scoring**
- Completing a clean resonance cascade through `3+` objects: `+60`
- Completing a cascade through a strong size-gradient lane: `+100 perspective bonus`
- Using `3` different anatomy modes in one uninterrupted performance phrase: `+120 contradiction bonus`
- Keeping the judge in high `INTEREST` without triggering `ALARM` for `10` seconds: `+80 composure bonus`
- Hitting a `SPUR` accent that lands on the final object of a cascade: `+40 sting flourish`
- Dropping objects into messy overlapping piles: `-20`
- Triggering a display burst with too few wetted objects: `-15 dead phrase`
- Filling the `ALARM` meter: run ends immediately

## **Difficulty Progression**
- Opening seconds: fewer objects, generous wet duration, and a patient judge teach the loop of place, wet, and perform.
- Mid run: object variety increases, useful gradients become less obvious, and the judge starts preferring cleaner, longer cascades over random noise.
- Late run: wet patches evaporate faster, rival debris rolls in and disturbs careful layouts, and repeated use of the same anatomy mode suffers diminishing returns.
- Final `15` seconds: the judge becomes excitable, `ALARM` rises faster, object spawn rate surges, and the highest scores come from risky multi-mode phrases executed on a nearly full stage.
